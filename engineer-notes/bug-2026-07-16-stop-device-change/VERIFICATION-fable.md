## Holes found (and whether they hold)

1. **The 64 KB-blocking reconciliation - pulled hard, does NOT break.** Frames climb because `frameCount += 1` happens in `deliver()` before the async enqueue; `send` never blocks the caller (`writeQueue.async`). After the pipe fills (~64 KB ≈ 1-2 s of 16 kHz mono ×2 streams), every subsequent frame becomes a pending work item on the unbounded serial DispatchQueue: ~64 KB/s × 26 min ≈ **~100 MB of RAM** - entirely plausible, no limit that would crash or drop, and invisible in every artifact. So "heartbeats healthy to 14484 frames + zero bytes consumed" is internally consistent. The RCA gestures at this in §7 ("whatever had backed up on Swift's writeQueue in memory") but never quantifies it; worth adding, not a defect.
2. **Meter-relay claim in Tier 1 is factually wrong** (see claims 6/8). The fix is free: cite the 0-byte `transcript_events.jsonl`, which is strictly stronger evidence than the one the RCA used.
3. **Minor timeline typo:** §2 says "~14484 heartbeats"; there are ~699 heartbeats (grep count), 14484 is the frames value. Cadence check (699 × ~2.24 s ≈ 1566 s vs duration 1569.89 s) actually corroborates the RCA's anchoring.
4. **"only catches EPIPE"** - imprecise (catches any thrown error); harmless because blocking throws nothing.
5. **Silence-before-read-loop proves less than it might appear** - a release build emits nothing pre-loop even on a healthy launch, so "zero Python lines" alone cannot distinguish import-wedge from read-loop-waiting. The RCA's actual discriminator (empty `audio/` despite bytes demonstrably entering the pipe) is the right one and holds: the first writes to an empty pipe cannot block, no write error was logged, so MSG_MEETING_START reached the pipe buffer; a child sitting in `read_exact` would have consumed it in milliseconds and created the WAVs. It didn't → the child never issued its first stdin read.
